# Warning Signs

A warning sign tells you what is coming before you can see it, so you have time to slow down. The exam
checks three things: what each picture means, how far ahead the sign stands, and what the sign doesn't do.

## How do I recognize a warning sign?

Look for a **triangle with a red border, point up**. The shape settles it: a sign that warns of something
ahead is a triangle.

![Five signs with a bicycle or motorcycle: a blue disc, a red-bordered triangle, two red rings and a small plate, numbered 1 to 5](../../../assets/questions/Question.38.pic.png)

*Only number 2, the triangle, warns: it is sign 1.24 "Intersection with a cycle path". The blue disc
orders, the red rings forbid, and the plate only adds information to another sign.*

> **Remember:** A warning sign warns and nothing more. It creates no duty to give way and it prohibits
> nothing. Sign 1.5 doesn't tell you to yield to a tram, and sign 1.24 doesn't tell you to yield to a
> cyclist. The priority rules decide who goes first.

## How far ahead does the sign stand?

Signs 1.1, 1.2 and 1.5 to 1.33 stand before the start of the dangerous section. The distance depends only
on where you are:

| Where | Distance from the sign to the danger |
|---|---|
| In a built-up area | 50 to 100 m |
| Outside built-up areas | 150 to 300 m |

So in a built-up area the danger begins at least 50 m after the sign, and outside at least 150 m after
it. If some other distance is needed, it is written on plate 8.1.1 "Distance to the object".

> **Common trap:** The danger doesn't begin at the sign. The gap is there to give you time to slow down.
> 300 to 500 m, 25 to 50 m and 100 to 150 m are not distances the Rules use for these signs.

## Two signs that break the triangle rule

| Sign | No. | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ![Long panel with red and white chevrons pointing left and right](../../../assets/signs/1.34.3.png) | 1.34.3 | Direction of the turn | Shows the ways you can go at a T-shaped intersection or a fork. It prohibits nothing |
| ![Grey square with a yellow border and a yellow cross](../../../assets/questions/Question.81.pic.png) | 1.35 | Intersection area | Marks an intersection with the yellow grid, marking 1.26 |

Sign 1.35 looks nothing like a warning sign, and it comes with a real rule. **Don't enter the intersection
if a jam ahead would force you to stop inside it** and block crossing traffic. Turning right or left is
the exception.

The condition matters. A jam ahead alone isn't enough: the ban applies when it would make you stop there.

Sign 1.35 stands at the boundary of the intersection. At a complex intersection, if it can't stand there,
it stands no more than 30 m from the boundary.

## Try it yourself

**You are on a road through the forest, outside a built-up area.**

![A forest road with a triangle showing a locomotive above a post with three red stripes](../../../assets/questions/Question.118.pic.png)

What is ahead? **A railway crossing without a barrier, 150 to 300 m on.** The locomotive means no barrier,
and this road is outside a built-up area. The sign says nothing about the number of tracks.

**You are in a built-up area and see this sign.**

![A wet town street with a triangle showing a skidding car](../../../assets/questions/Question.128.pic.png)

Where does the slippery section begin? **At least 50 m after the sign.** Not immediately after it, and not
at least 100 m: 50 m is the shortest distance used in a built-up area.

## Key points

- Warning signs are triangles with a red border. They warn, and they create no duty of their own.
- Built-up area: 50 to 100 m before the danger. Outside: 150 to 300 m. Plate 8.1.1 gives any other distance.
- 1.1 shows a barrier, 1.2 a locomotive without one. Only the crossed boards 1.3 tell you the tracks.
- 1.6 is a plain cross of equal arms: neither road is the main one.
- 1.31 warns of a tunnel with no artificial lighting or a hard-to-see entrance.
- Under 1.35, don't enter if a jam would force you to stop inside. It stands no more than 30 m from the boundary.
