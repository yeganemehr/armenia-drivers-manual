# Review notes: 06 Accidents and First Aid

Main text follows the questions. Each line is a point where question guidance looks outdated, unsafe or inconsistent. Popups named here carry the accepted guidance.

- #565, #594: pressing an artery's pressure point looks outdated. Current first-aid guidance relies on firm direct pressure on the wound. Popup added in 02-bleeding-wounds.
- #592: the 1 hour / half hour tourniquet limits could lead a reader to remove a tourniquet. Current guidance is not to loosen or remove it and to leave that to medical staff. Popup added in 02-bleeding-wounds, and it pulls against the main text, so please review.
- #594: "as close to the wound as possible" gives no gap. Courses usually teach a few centimetres above the wound and not over a joint. Left out of the popup.
- #556: raising the limb is no longer a required step in much current guidance, which puts direct pressure first. #556 also says washing "can introduce infection", which sits badly with #585.
- #561: the explanation lists "stop the bleeding by direct pressure" as wrong for an open fracture. Heavy bleeding still needs control, by pressing around the bone. Popup added in 03-fractures-burns.
- #577: removing a fallen wire with an insulating object looks unsafe. Dry wood does not reliably protect against high voltage, and the ground nearby may be live. Accepted guidance is to stay well back until the power is confirmed off. It also pulls against #559 (don't approach). Main text ties the two together, and a popup was added in 03-fractures-burns.
- #588: "lay the casualty on their back on a hard, flat surface" conflicts with "do not change the position". Current guidance is to keep the person still as found and support the head. Popup added in 03-fractures-burns.
- #590: the explanation says a cervical splint is not prohibited. Current guidance advises first aiders against applying cervical collars, including improvised ones. Not taught in the main text.
- #590: direct pressure on a head wound needs care over a possible depressed skull fracture. Popup added in 02-bleeding-wounds.
- #590, #588: the recovery position for a head injury and "do not move" for a suspected spine injury are never reconciled, though a head injury can involve the neck.
- #595: "prohibited" with "no such exception" is stricter than accepted first aid, which has people use their own inhaler and has aspirin for suspected heart attack when no contraindication applies. Asthma inhaler popup added in 04-unconscious-casualty. Aspirin left out.
- #595, #575: #595 prohibits medicines while #575 requires sugar, which is fine, but #575 relies on the casualty being awake. The question doesn't say so. Main text adds the limit from #573 and #595.
- #575: "loss of consciousness within minutes" overstates how fast low blood sugar usually progresses.
- #568: 60-80 beats per minute is narrower than the usual adult range of 60-100, so 80-100 is not abnormal in itself as the explanation suggests.
- #569: 12-18 is slightly narrower than the usual adult range of 12-20.
- #578: "only with sterile gloves" is stricter than needed. Clean disposable gloves are normal practice, and the word "only" could put someone off helping. The explanation does allow any barrier, and main text follows that.
- #600: "if possible apply cold" could be read as ice on a burn, which accepted guidance rules out. Popup added in 03-fractures-burns. Water to drink suits only a fully awake casualty.
- #589: a warm drink suits only a fully awake casualty. Warming frostbite is also advised against where it may refreeze.
- #566, #560, #551: the order puts breathing before severe bleeding. Some current protocols treat catastrophic bleeding first. The main text keeps the question order.
- #560: the ambulance call comes after the check. Accepted practice has a bystander call straight away. Popup added in 01-after-a-crash.
- #570, #599: #570's explanation says a casualty is moved only when the scene is dangerous, while #599 adds hypothermia, loss of consciousness and breathing, and first aid being impossible in the cabin. The phrase "loss of consciousness and breathing" is also unclear: it could mean either one or both.
- #558, #573: #558 goes straight to raising the legs of a collapsed person, while #573 puts opening the airway first. Popup added in 04-unconscious-casualty.
- #552: REVIEW.md item. Main text says "press the nose" and leaves out which part. The soft part is in a popup.
- #554: REVIEW.md item. Main text says not to restrain and to let the convulsions end, and omits both "do not touch" and "protect the head". Protecting the head is in a popup.
- #585, #597: REVIEW.md item. Main text lists the cases separately, with #586 and #597 as the default at an accident, and says "less dangerous" isn't defined.
